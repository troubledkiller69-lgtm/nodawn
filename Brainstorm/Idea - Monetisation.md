# Idea - Monetisation

**The pitch:** sell identity, never capability. The raid's tension is scarcity; anything that reduces scarcity for money removes the game. The store sells clothes, finishes, furniture and gestures. It never sells safety, time or power.

## 1. The one rule

Every item must answer **no** to: *does this help the buyer survive, extract, carry, aim, heal, hide, or earn faster?* If yes, it does not ship.

The test is not "is it cosmetic?" It is "does it change a fight or a haul?" A backpack skin passes. A bigger backpack fails. A darker jacket passes only inside the value band in §4. A quieter boot fails.

## 2. The firewall

**Dollars and Robux never touch.**

Dollars are pre-war US cash, symbol `$`, code `USD`. The prison honors them because the outside traders still do; the vendors price food, water, ammo and gear in the same note.

- Dollars are earned in raid and spent on gear, ammo and attachments.
- Robux buys appearance.
- No Dollars can be bought with Robux. No Robux item grants Dollars. No cosmetic sells for Dollars. No crate contains either.
- No third currency. A premium currency is a dark pattern and a second ledger to exploit.

If the two economies ever meet, the game is pay-to-win and the promise is dead.

## 3. What the game refuses to sell

| Refused | Why |
|---|---|
| Stash / vault expansion | Direct haul advantage. The vault is the scoreboard. |
| Secure container / insurance | Removes the loss that makes extraction mean something. |
| XP / loot / currency boosters | Sells time, which is the only thing a new player has. |
| Weapons, ammo, armour, attachments | The raid is the only place gear should come from. |
| Extra extracts, revive tokens, private raid servers | Sells safety. |
| Trader reputation | Access is earned, not bought. |
| Queue priority, extra server slots | Sells other players' time. |
| Cosmetics that change silhouette, reflectivity, footstep audio or sights | A cosmetic that hides you is a power-up. |
| Loot boxes / paid random items | Gambling, and Roblox policy. |
| Paid access at launch | Splits the population an extraction shooter cannot afford. |

## 4. The cosmetic contract

The game is first-person, permanently night, foggy. A cosmetic that reads at 80 studs in fog is a different object from one that reads in a store thumbnail. These rules are art direction and balance at the same time.

- **Silhouette envelope.** Every outfit uses the same body and the same gear volume. Jackets may vary within a fixed shoulder width and hem band. No hoods up, no ghillie, no helmets, no backpacks that add outline. The survivor creator's base clothing is the floor; cosmetics layer over it.
- **Value band.** All fabrics sit inside a fixed luminance range under hub light. No pure black, no pure white, no environment-matching camo. The default is already the best camouflage in the game; that is fine.
- **Weapon finishes.** Same mesh, same sights, same roughness and metalness. Albedo and pattern only. No 3D wraps, no pure black, no glowing parts. A polished finish is a self-inflicted disadvantage and is allowed.
- **No audio cosmetics.** Footsteps, gunshots, reloads, muzzle flash and tracers are information. They are not for sale.
- **Emotes.** Default tactical gestures — wave, point, surrender, beckon — are free. Paid emotes are social: salute, smoke, laugh, pray, a hand on the chest. Same duration, same visibility. No paid emote is quieter or faster than a free one.
- **Never lost.** Cosmetics are account-bound. Death shows a copy on the corpse; the killer cannot loot it. A dog tag is a separate social item and can drop, but the tag's design is not lost.

## 5. What is sold

| Category | Examples | Seen in | Price band |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outfit | raincoat, work jacket, prison blues, scavenger layers | hub, raid silhouette | 199–399 |
| Gloves / sleeves / boots | first-person visible | raid, hub | 49–149 |
| Weapon finish | parkerised, prison gray, worn bluing, tape and paint | first-person | 99–249 |
| Charm / baseplate | small, matte, no glow | first-person | 49–99 |
| Cell decor pack | bunk, shelf, poster (no text), radio, curtain, plant | hub cell | 149–349 |
| Emote pack | 4–6 hand gestures | hub, raid | 99–199 |
| Radio pack | original ambient/industrial loops | hub cell | 149–299 |
| Dog tag design | shape and colour; the name is UI | death drop | 49–99 |
| Name / appearance change | convenience | UI | 99–199 |
| Supporter pack | one-time bundle | all | 799–999 |
| Seasonal pass | free + paid cosmetic track | all | 399–499 |

Prices are Roblox-native, not Steam-native. A 399-Robux outfit is an impulse buy for a Roblox player; a 1,500-Robux outfit is a decision. Volume is the model.

## 6. The Commissary

The store is diegetic: a **commissary** in the hub, a fenced counter with a clerk and a catalog. The world stays textless; the catalog is UI. No price tags, no signs, no floating labels.

The Commissary sells **personal effects**. The gear vendors sell tools of survival. Two counters, two currencies, no overlap. A player should learn within one minute which one is which.

The **wardrobe** is in the cell, next to a mirror. The mirror is the only place a player sees the full outfit; it is a camera rig, not a gameplay feature. The cell is the stage. The catwalk, the yard and the mess hall are where other players see it.

## 7. The two anchor products

**First Night — supporter pack, 799–999 Robux.**

- One outfit (a raincoat layer, within the envelope).
- One weapon finish ("Prison Gray").
- One cell pack (bunk, shelf, radio).
- One dog tag.
- Name in the credits (UI, not the world).
- No Dollars. No gear. No XP. No early access.

**Season 1 pass — 399–499 Robux.**

- 50 tiers, 8–10 weeks.
- Free track: 15–20 cosmetic items, including one full outfit.
- Paid track: 25–30 items; no power, no boosters, no currency.
- Progress comes from renown ([[Idea - Progression]]), not from purchases.
- Items return in the archive after the season. No permanent missables, no countdown pressure.

## 8. The hub is the storefront

In raid, a cosmetic is a glimpse at 40 studs in fog. In the hub, it is a person standing next to you at the vendor. The hub is the only place the store can work.

That means the hub's social density is a monetisation feature, not a luxury:

- The cell block catwalk, the yard and the mess hall need places to stand, lean and talk.
- Warm practical light against the fog; the value band in §4 is tested there.
- A radio, a fire barrel, a card table — props that make players linger.
- No text, no ads, no storefront signage. The Commissary is found, not advertised.

## 9. Roblox economics

- **Game passes** for permanent cosmetics, the supporter pack and each season's pass. No developer products at launch, because there is no consumable currency.
- Roblox takes its platform cut, and DevEx takes another. Model at roughly a fifth to a third of player spend reaching the developer; price for volume, not margin.
- **Premium payouts** reward retention. A hub players return to is worth more than a store they scroll once.
- **No paid random items.** Direct purchase, visible item, known price.
- **No UGC/limiteds dependency.** Own the items, keep the catalog small.
- **No paid access.** The game needs a populated raid at every hour.

## 10. If revenue is short

Add **categories**, not power:

- More cell decor: curtains, bedding, posters, a plant, a shelf of found objects.
- More first-person cosmetics: glove patterns, sleeve patches, boot colour, weapon charms.
- More finishes: wear stages, tape, paint, parkerising.
- A tip jar with a cosmetic badge.

Do not add stash tabs, insurance, boosters, "convenience" loadout slots, or a premium currency. The first bad item is the one that teaches players the promise is negotiable.

## 11. The promise

Store page and launch post: **"Nothing in this store keeps you alive."**

Then prove it: every item is inspectable, every stat is unchanged, and the patch notes say so. The promise is the marketing. In a genre where editions sell stash space and safety, a cosmetic-only extraction shooter is a position, not an apology.

## 12. Open questions

- Does the outfit layer over the survivor creator's clothing, or replace it? **Recommend layer.**
- Is the mirror in the cell, or a wardrobe UI? **Recommend a physical mirror; it sells the outfit.**
- Do supporters get a permanent marker? **A small patch, not an aura.**
- Launch catalog size? **12–16 items.** A small, confident store beats a wall of filler.
- Is the pass seasonal or always-on? **Seasonal, archive returns.**
- Do we sell name colours? **Later, if at all; social, not power.**
