Treat the entries below as a long-term selection pool. Choose items by distinct role and provenance rather than implementing all fifty at once.

## Attachment principles

- Keep the existing trade-offs: suppressors reduce noise but add weight and handling cost; compensators reduce recoil while increasing noise; lights aid dark interiors while revealing the user.
- Only offer attachments on a weapon with a real compatible interface. Do not add rails to classic, unmodified guns. A threaded barrel, adapter, clamp, or period-correct mount must be represented where needed.
- Optics should require a suitable mount; default to usable irons. Scopes belong on rifles built for them. No lasers, damage boosts, or universal best-in-slot attachments.
- Possible slot families: muzzle (where threaded), optic (where mounted), light (rail/clamp where plausible), sling (visual/handling choice), and magazine (only authentic patterns/capacities). Not every weapon uses every slot.
- Treat the sets in the tables as plausible example builds, subject to model-specific verification before implementation. “None” is a deliberate and often historically correct configuration.
- Keep the project's firearm rule: research the exact variant before building its dimensions, controls, ammunition, action, capacity, reload, sights, and attachments.

### Firearm role coverage

- **Sidearms:** 1–6.
- **Quiet/utility:** 7 and suppressed variants where compatible.
- **Shotguns:** 8–9.
- **Common scavenger rifles:** 10, 13, 15, 20.
- **Military rifles:** 11–12, 14, 16–17.
- **Precision/hunting:** 18–22.
- **Compact automatics:** 23–25.

Avoid shipping near-duplicates together unless their ammunition, source, cadence, capacity, handling, or attachment compatibility creates a meaningful choice. Several items deliberately overlap with the existing roster; this is a selection pool, not a demand to add them all.

## Suggested selection path

1. Keep [[Idea - Weapon Roster]]'s existing launch scope intact.
2. For the next roster review, select 4–6 additions that fill a real gap: one quiet utility firearm, one common pump alternative only if needed, one distinct precision rifle, one accessible blunt tool, one utility blade and one long-reach melee.
3. Verify each candidate's exact model and variant before creating a build spec. Attachment compatibility in this note is a research prompt, not final asset authorization.
4. Add a new weapon only when its loot provenance, ammunition, sound, handling, capacity, and player choice are distinct enough to justify another item.
5. Keep attachment stock scarce and tied to provenance; no universal vendor inventory.
